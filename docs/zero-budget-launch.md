# Zero-Budget Launch Plan

Open this when preparing or running Plainstride's first public launch, recruiting early runners, writing outreach, or evaluating early acquisition and retention.

## Objective

Treat the first release as a learning launch rather than a mass-market launch.

The initial goal is to find 25–50 runners who:

- complete at least two activities with Plainstride
- return for another recommendation
- can explain the benefit in their own words
- would be disappointed if the product disappeared

Downloads, followers, impressions, and launch-day attention are supporting indicators. They are not the primary definition of success.

## Positioning

### Primary promise

> Plainstride tells you what run makes sense today—and adapts as your life and training change.

### Initial audience

Focus on recreational runners who want consistency or have a goal but do not want to design and manage their own training plan.

Start by observing these motivations separately:

- training for a 5K, 10K, or half marathon
- trying to run consistently
- returning after a break
- wanting encouragement or companionship
- wanting a trusted person to follow a live run

Let activation and retention determine which segment becomes the primary audience. Do not broaden the positioning to every sport or every person who exercises.

### Message hierarchy

1. Know what run makes sense today.
2. The plan adapts when life changes.
3. Build momentum without managing a training spreadsheet.
4. Track progress, run with guidance, and stay connected to people you trust.

Avoid leading with `AI-powered`, a long feature list, or a comparison with Strava. AI is an implementation detail unless a runner describes it as the reason they care.

## Founder-Led Growth Loop

1. Personally recruit a runner.
2. Help the runner reach the first recommended activity.
3. Learn what was useful, confusing, or missing.
4. Turn the learning into a product improvement or an authentic story.
5. Invite the runner to refer one appropriate friend after a successful experience.

Founder access is an advantage for the first cohort. Make it easy for early runners to reply directly with feedback.

## Preparation During App Review

- Create a one-page site with one promise, a short demonstration, three benefits, the founder story, and the App Store link.
- Record five short demonstrations:
  - choosing a run when only 30 minutes are available
  - adjusting after a missed workout
  - moving from readiness to today's recommendation
  - reviewing progress after a run
  - sharing a live run with a trusted person
- Make a list of 50–100 potential early runners, grouped by relationship strength and likely motivation.
- Prepare a direct feedback route and a lightweight interview note template.
- Create a distinct App Store campaign link for each meaningful source, such as close network, acquaintances, running groups, Instagram, and community posts.
- Confirm that the first three App Store screenshots communicate outcomes:
  1. Know what to run today
  2. A plan that adapts with you
  3. Build momentum without training anxiety

## First 30 Days

### Week 1: recruit the first 20 runners

Contact people individually. Personalize every message with the actual relationship or reason Plainstride may be relevant.

Ask each interested person to:

- try Plainstride for two runs within one week
- share anything confusing as it happens
- have a 10–15 minute conversation afterward, if willing

Do not ask for a rating or referral before they have experienced a satisfying outcome.

### Week 2: identify the strongest segment

Review behavior and interviews by runner motivation. Determine:

- who completes onboarding
- who accepts the recommended workout
- who completes an activity
- who returns within seven days
- which users complete at least two activities
- what language repeat users use to describe the benefit

Choose one leading segment for the next month. Update outreach and App Store creative around its job rather than around demographics alone.

### Week 3: publish proof and useful ideas

Turn recurring runner questions into useful content. Examples:

- What should I run after sleeping badly?
- Missing one workout should not destroy a training plan.
- How should I return after two weeks off?
- Why do new runners run easy days too hard?
- What early runners are teaching me while I build Plainstride.

Reuse each strong idea as a short video, written post, product demonstration, and helpful community answer. Concentrate on no more than two channels. Participate in communities as a member; follow their promotional rules and avoid repetitive link dropping.

### Week 4: introduce referrals

Ask satisfied runners to invite one person who would genuinely benefit. Prefer natural social objects—shared activities, progress, live-run links, and Together invitations—over rewards or referral points.

Suggested framing:

> I'm using Plainstride for this week's running plan. Want to keep each other consistent?

Measure invitations that result in an activated runner, not just invitations sent.

## Outreach Principles

- Ask for use and honest feedback, not praise.
- Explain why the product may be relevant to that specific person.
- Give a clear, small commitment: two runs in one week.
- Make declining easy and never chase someone repeatedly.
- Do not ask a barely known contact for a review, public endorsement, or introductions.
- Send at most one follow-up when there is no response.
- Replace `[App Store link]` before sending.

## Outreach Messages

These are starting points. Add one personal sentence whenever possible.

### Close friend

> Hey! Big milestone: Plainstride, the running app I've been building, is now live. Would you help me test the real first-user experience? I'd love for you to use it for two runs in one week and tell me honestly what feels useful, confusing, or unnecessary. No need to be nice—I need the friend version of brutally honest. Here's the link: [App Store link]

### Friend

> Hey! I've been building an iPhone running app called Plainstride, and it is now available on the App Store. It recommends what run makes sense today and adapts the plan when your schedule or readiness changes. Since you run, would you be open to trying it for two runs and sharing honest feedback afterward? Absolutely no pressure, but it would help me a lot at this early stage. [App Store link]

### Acquaintance

> Hi [Name]—I remember that you're into running, so I wanted to reach out. I've built an iPhone app called Plainstride that helps recreational runners decide what to run today and adjusts their plan when life gets in the way. I'm looking for a small group of early users willing to try it for two runs and tell me what works or doesn't. If that sounds relevant, I'd be grateful to have you try it; no worries at all if the timing isn't right. [App Store link]

### Barely known contact

> Hi [Name]—we [met at / know each other through / briefly connected via] [context]. I noticed that you run, and I hope you don't mind the direct message. I've built an iPhone app called Plainstride that suggests what run makes sense today and adapts around a runner's schedule and readiness. I'm inviting a small number of recreational runners to try it for two runs and give candid feedback. If that is genuinely useful to you, here's the link: [App Store link]. If not, no need to respond—and I won't keep following up.

## Follow-Up Messages

### Interested but has not started

> Hey—quick Plainstride follow-up. If you still want to try it, the most helpful test is simply to complete onboarding and use it for two runs. If now isn't a good time, no problem at all.

### After the first run

> Thanks for giving Plainstride a real try. While it's fresh: what was the most useful part, and where did you hesitate or feel confused? Even a one-line answer helps.

### After two runs

> You've now given Plainstride the kind of test I really needed—thank you. Could I ask you three quick questions? What would make you keep using it, what would make you delete it, and how would you describe it to another runner?

### Referral request after a positive experience

> I'm really glad Plainstride has been useful. If one runner comes to mind who also struggles with knowing what to run or staying consistent, would you feel comfortable inviting them? One well-matched person is much more helpful than a broad post. No pressure.

### Single no-response follow-up for an acquaintance

> Hi [Name]—one quick follow-up in case my earlier message got buried. I'm still looking for a few runners to try Plainstride for two runs and give honest feedback. No worries if it isn't relevant or the timing is bad; I won't follow up again. [App Store link]

## Interview Prompts

Keep early interviews conversational. Ask for examples rather than hypothetical feature requests.

- What were you hoping Plainstride would help you do?
- What did you expect after onboarding?
- Was today's recommendation easy to trust and understand?
- Where did you hesitate or feel confused?
- What did you do instead when Plainstride was not helpful?
- What would make you open it before your next run?
- What would make you delete it?
- How would you describe Plainstride to another runner?
- How disappointed would you be if it disappeared: very, somewhat, or not disappointed?

Do not promise every requested feature. Look for repeated problems and behavior.

## Measurement

Review a compact weekly scorecard:

- App Store impressions, page views, downloads, and conversion by source
- onboarding completion rate
- recommended workout viewed
- first activity completed
- second activity completed within seven days
- week-one return rate
- invitations sent, accepted, and activated
- interview count and repeated themes
- users who would be very disappointed if Plainstride disappeared

Segment these measures by acquisition source and runner motivation. A small source producing repeat runners is more valuable than a large source producing downloads only.

## App Store Iteration

- Respond promptly and constructively to reviews.
- Ask for a rating only after a satisfying moment, such as a completed activity or visible progress.
- Test the first screenshot and value proposition before less important creative details.
- Wait for sufficient traffic before trusting product-page experiment results.
- Create custom product pages later for distinct proven intentions, such as race preparation, consistency, returning after a break, or trusted live sharing.

## Funding Gate

Do not make venture funding a prerequisite for validating the launch. Consider fundraising when Plainstride can demonstrate:

- a clearly identifiable retained segment
- improving activation and retention
- organic referrals that produce activated runners
- testimonials about a repeated benefit
- early willingness to pay
- a credible use of capital that accelerates something already working

Until then, prioritize conversations, retention, product learning, and evidence over reach.
